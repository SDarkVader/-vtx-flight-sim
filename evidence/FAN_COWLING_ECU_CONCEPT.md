# Fan and cowling stability ECU — concept and advisory prototype

Pilot commands remain authoritative requests. The proposed controller coordinates four fan speeds, four blade collectives and four two-axis cowling tilt actuators to achieve those requests while accounting for rotor thrust and torque, aircraft motion, actuator limits, engine power, and component health. It is an envelope-protection and control-allocation concept, not autopilot and not a guarantee that an unrecoverable state can always be prevented.

## Adjustable training assistance

Provide a visible 0–100% stability-assistance setting in training. At 0%, optional damping assistance is disabled; increasing the setting blends in progressively more verified roll, pitch and yaw rate damping. Pilot requests remain present throughout. The scale must not change the separate equipment-envelope and fault-monitoring limits; if protection intervenes, the display must identify it. Assistance levels are not guarantees of aircraft stability. Level changes should be smoothly blended with actuator-aware rate limits, and fault or saturation handling takes priority over training preferences.

Before enabling this in flight simulation, design and validate a multi-axis control allocator using the aircraft's measured thrust/torque response and actual actuator behavior. Measure handling and stability with assistance at 0%, intermediate levels and 100%, including low altitude, asymmetric fan thrust, insufficient power, sensor faults and actuator saturation. The current advisory code does not implement damping, interpolation, an allocator or protection intervention; there is no working training slider yet.

Each cowling should support a full 360° tilt direction with resultant tilt no greater than the requested 20°; this is a cone, not independent 20° limits on X and Y. The existing simulator already validates this cone for commands. Fan spin direction remains determined by each branch's configured counter-rotating sense; rotating the cowling tilt direction does not reverse blade spin. Cabin leveling and cabin yaw remain separate gimbal functions.

The ECU must observe actual fan speed and acceleration, collective and cowling angle and rate, motor current/torque/temperature, engine power and speed, and aircraft roll/pitch/yaw rates, attitude and vertical motion. It needs validity and timestamp monitoring, independent limit enforcement, and a reasoned degraded mode for sensor, actuator, motor, power-bus and control-computer faults. It should continuously estimate available thrust and power and limit command rate or coordinate other fans where verified to avoid excessive angular acceleration, loss of lift, overspeed, power demand and structural loads. Do not assume simply equalizing fan speeds is stabilizing: pitch, tilt and local flow change thrust and moments. Give the pilot feedback whenever a request is limited and why. Fault-specific handling must be established by analysis, not a blanket instruction to shut down or lock a fan.

`tools/fan_ecu_shadow.py` is a deliberately advisory-only first step: it flags individually measured tilt beyond the 20° cone, checks rpm only if an externally specified validated limit is supplied, and reports speed spread without judging whether it is safe. It does not modify live controls, estimate stability, test actuator rates, or protect an aircraft. Its two focused checks cover combined-axis cone behavior, optional rpm thresholds and non-finite input rejection. The simulator's current equality constraints link fan speeds, so independent-drive allocation is not yet represented. Integrate only after the independent drive and its actuator dynamics, sensor validity and coupled loads have been modeled, then test on the ground and in controlled simulations, including failed actuators and saturated power. Do not use assumed 1,100–1,300 rpm calculations as actual limits.
