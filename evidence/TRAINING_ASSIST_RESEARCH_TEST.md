# Training assistance research and simulation checks

The simulator now includes a 0–100% experimental **shadow** rate-damping study. It computes small candidate collective adjustments from exact simulated body rates, limited to ±0.5° per fan and preserving opposing pair totals and existing 0–30° limits. The candidate is visible to the user and in telemetry but is **not sent to actuators**. The actual flight controls remain the pilot's commands. The 20° combined cowling command limit remains in force. The real independent fan-speed transmission, cowling stabilization, yaw damping, sensor noise/fault handling, allocator, power envelope, and active flight protections are not implemented.

At upright nacelles and a fixed 1,200 rpm reference, a static aerodynamic moment calculation using modeled thrust curves gave approximately −178 N m at 50% and −356 N m at 100% for a +10°/s hypothetical roll or pitch rate. Wolfram evaluated the basic north/south moment expression as `−2 d L`. These confirm a sign and scaling in that particular static case, not dynamic stability or the correctness of the simplifications across tilt and flow.

An isolated, synthetic one-second airborne dynamic bench is recorded in `training_assist_dynamic_check.json` with reproducible source `tools/bench_rate_assist_dynamics.py`. Its initial airborne position, angular velocity, rotor speed and 10° collective are explicitly set as test fixtures; it is not a piloted flight or trimmed hover. For its nominal pitch-axis case, the observed body pitch rate after one second was about 10.57°/s at 0%, 10.88°/s at 50%, and 11.17°/s at 100%. The aircraft was descending and the deliberately simplified initial state has constraint transients. The comparison does not isolate a root cause or prove the aircraft would behave this way throughout its envelope; nevertheless it does **not demonstrate improved damping** and fails the acceptance criterion for an active controller. The private bench explicitly enables the candidate; the web API has no active-control switch. The candidate is sampled at 20 Hz to bound processing overhead.

Headless Chromium verified the pre-shadow UI wiring at 0%, 50% and 100%, with the values reaching simulator telemetry. Automated tests check symmetry, sign, range limits, invalid values, and that the production aircraft does not apply unvalidated candidate commands. This is not an assurance of crash-free behavior, flight stability, or real-aircraft functionality.

Research: [Ivler and Juhász, Evaluation of Control Allocation Techniques for a Medium Lift Tilt-Rotor](https://doi.org/10.4050/f-0071-2015-10169), compares allocation with time/frequency and robustness metrics, including a piloted simulation. [Bachler et al., Jerk-Based Control Allocation for Aerial Vehicles With Tilting Rotors](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4070054), discusses actuator rate saturation and bandwidth. These are related research, not validation of this aircraft or the candidate controller. Undermind and SciSpace were searched; Elicit returned an account API-access denial. Acumen's results did not yield directly applicable validated controller constants. Zotero Desktop was not available in this environment. No literature-derived gain has been asserted.

Next test must establish a trimmed state without initial constraint impulses, measure closed-loop response and actuator saturation over multiple roll/pitch/yaw and tilt conditions, then design a coupled allocator accounting for changing thrust, rotor gyroscopic dynamics, power, noise and failures. A safe controller is not established by a favorable one-second run.
