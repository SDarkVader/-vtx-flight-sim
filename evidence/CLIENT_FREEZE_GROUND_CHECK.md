# Client responsiveness and low-power ground check

The client was reported to freeze repeatedly. This change addresses two concrete workload risks, not a confirmed single root cause: synchronous mesh preparation and graphics-buffer retention while loading a roughly 12 MB/731-mesh aircraft, and replay reading and sending an entire flight audit while blocking the async physics server. Model batching now yields between work chunks, waits to render the scene until batching is done, and disposes temporary buffers and old assets. Replay runs on a worker thread and keeps up to 1,200 sampled frames including endpoints; original logs remain intact and the interface discloses sampling.

The largest available 30 MB uncompressed flight log contained 3,428 audit frames; local replay processing returned 858 frames in approximately 0.65 seconds. A headless Chromium check at 390 × 844 using software rendering loaded the model, started a 0.1× run, and advanced from 0.00 to 1.19 simulated seconds over 12 seconds without JavaScript exceptions. Browser long-task observation saw four tasks, with a maximum of 545 ms, so this is not proof of freeze-free performance on a phone or other hardware. A favicon 404 was observed and is unrelated to the flight code.

For a separate low-power check, a live Colin-authority run requested governor levels 5%, 10%, and 15% in stages, with all four collectives feathered. Requests were accepted; at the third observation the simulated time was 0.872 s, the four coupled rotors were approximately 172.5 rpm, radar clearance 0.753 m, and no crash or solver error was reported. The run ended on the ground with no takeoff. Run ID `20261031T164741-4cdb7b`. The short ground test did not exercise the proposed independent fan transmissions or adjustable stability assistance, which are not implemented in this simulator. It cannot establish flight stability, performance, or crash-free behavior over long sessions.
