# Four individually controlled fan transmissions — preliminary concept

Status: architecture and packaging study, not an engineered gearbox, flight-qualified design, or an installed modification. The original VTX P0-V4 Blendstra assembly remains unchanged. The illustration generated by `tools/model_four_variators.py` is a separate concept model: gear teeth, power lines, envelopes, mounting, lubrication, clearances and structural load paths are schematic. The displayed housings do not demonstrate mesh, kinematics, actual physical fit or 20° articulation clearance. The live simulator still has a common fixed-ratio rotor coupling; it does not yet provide independent speed control.

## Objective and boundary

Drive each of the four fans at an independently commanded speed from the existing common 6,016 rpm power-turbine reference. Retain the alternating clockwise/anticlockwise rotor senses and fan articulation about the upper shaft joint. This is a new transmission hypothesis, not a claim that the present P03 gearing already supplies independent output speed. No automatic attitude controller or hidden rotor pose changes are implied.

Candidate per branch: common source → four-way power distribution and fixed nominal 4:1 reduction → differential planetary → fan output at the carrier, sun driven by the reducer, ring connected to a bidirectional motor-generator and power electronics. Two branches require an appropriate reversing stage to maintain the existing opposite fan rotation. Gear meshing and reversing stage geometry are not specified by this concept illustration. Each motor-generator and inverter exchanges power through a shared DC bus, with a suitably sized source/sink, protection, isolation and thermal management. An electronic controller sets each ring speed/torque from its own rotor-speed target and measured speed, within shared engine, electrical, gear, thermal and rotor limits. Pitch and fan tilt remain independently actuated. A speed reference is not a guarantee of available thrust or torque.

For a simple planetary with an externally meshed sun and internal ring with 20 and 60 teeth respectively, the kinematic relation is `(omega_s + 3 omega_r)/4 = omega_c`. At the assumed 6,016 rpm turbine output and 4:1 fixed reducer, the sun rotates at 1,504 rpm. The carrier is the fan output. Nominal 1,200 rpm requires ring speed 288 rpm. For illustrative 1,100 / 1,200 / 1,300 rpm fan speeds the ring speeds are -112 / 288 / 688 rpm respectively, in the branch's normalized direction. The 1,100–1,300 range is a calculation exercise, not an approved operating envelope. Shaft direction, mesh, phase, loads, inertia and controls must be designed for each physical branch.

## Power and torque accounting

At an assumed total 300 kW mechanical input, equal branches and 95% overall mechanical transfer efficiency, output is 71.25 kW per fan. At 1,200 rpm that is approximately 566.99 N·m per fan. The turbine output torque is 476.19 N·m total, or 119.05 N·m per equal branch. These are steady operating-point values, not peak ratings. The simulation also has a separate 469.8 kW allowed engine limit and accessory/servo loads; real installed curves and loss maps are not established. Allocate power to accessories first, then enforce a combined four-branch budget. Unequal load distribution, acceleration and emergency torques need separate sizing; never allocate the full turbine power to each branch.

For a *lossless, steady-state illustrative* 20:60 planetary with carrier output magnitude fixed at 566.99 N·m, ring torque magnitude is 3/4 of carrier magnitude, or approximately 425.24 N·m. Multiplying this torque magnitude by the signed ring speed gives -4.99 / +12.83 / +30.64 kW at the illustrative 1,100 / 1,200 / 1,300 rpm carrier points. Signs describe ring speed under a fixed torque-sign convention; actual motoring/regeneration and required DC-bus power depend on the complete torque and power sign convention and losses. The bus must handle net flow, branch imbalance, transients and an energy sink/source; there is no assumed free energy. Rotor torque itself changes with RPM, pitch and flow, so these are not a drive duty cycle or motor rating. Recalculate all cases with the actual polar, control excursions, braking, line losses and transient inertia.

The 4:1 first stage and 20:60 tooth counts are an illustrative mathematical split, not an optimized tooth count, practical housing diameter, final efficiency, weight or manufactured gear. The planetary fixed-annulus relationship, bearing loads and tooth geometry must be independently checked before detailing. Real freewheel/one-way clutch location and autorotation behaviour must be worked out for both counter-rotating senses and independent branch failures; no automatic lock or disconnect behaviour is asserted.

## Required design work before installed flight simulation

1. Determine allowable speed bands, rotor critical speeds, maximum overspeed, inertias, and transient torque/load envelopes from the existing geometry and measured or defensible blade data. Keep 800/1200 rpm conditions and the previous power study distinct from a new certified envelope.
2. Specify the common shaft architecture and gear topology, directions, bearing reactions, shaft torque, four branch transmissions, clutching, containment, lubrication, oil cooling, seals and maintainability. Demonstrate a packaging fit with the actual latest assembly, ±20° fan motion, supports, shafts, cabin, and adjacent housings. The concept drawing is not that check.
3. Size each motor-generator, inverter, DC bus, source/sink and cooling for four-quadrant power exchange, transients and failure cases; account for all conversion and gear losses and added mass/CG/inertia. A failed inverter, common bus or seized gearbox must not be treated as harmless.
4. Develop independent speed measurements, command rate and torque limits, motor current control, common-power arbitration, rotor overspeed/underspeed response, safe degraded operation, and pilot indication. Verify stability with rotor, flexible shaft, electric and control-loop dynamics; quantify common-mode failures, cross-coupling and autorotation.
5. Bench test one branch first with independent sensor traces of rotor torque, shaft speed and electrical/mechanical power. Only after energy accounting and actual speed separation are verified should four branches be added to a separate simulated experiment with regression tests for spin direction, no shared-speed constraints, total power, fuel and reaction torque. The old shared-shaft model must remain available as the historical baseline.
